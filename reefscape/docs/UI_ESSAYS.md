# REEFSCAPE — page essays

These paragraphs used to be commented out inside `web/index.html`. They are the reasoning behind the
numbers the page prints: measurements, named scripts, and the builds they were taken on. Nothing here is
rendered by the page. Kept verbatim, in page order.

## Planner decisions

Only filled in when an ALLIANCE is running the Planner. It is the log of the analytic model's re-solves: when it re-planned, what it measured for the fleet's pace, and when it moved the endgame commit. Read it against the field — a commit that lands at 6 s left is the same answer the **Numbers** measurements give every MATCH, and one that does not is the planner reacting to what that MATCH actually left on the REEF.


## Piece flow

The ledger the score does not keep. **CORAL drawn** is one piece into the hopper, split by where it came from: a STATION queue is what the HUMAN PLAYER feeds, a CORAL MARK is one the staging put on the FIELD and nobody has handled yet, and a piece off the CARPET is one somebody failed to keep — the four source rows add up to the row above them, so a fleet that draws half its CORAL off the carpet is cleaning up after itself, which is a different problem from a slow elevator. **Grabs that finished with the piece gone** and **placements that found the branch gone** are the two ways a cycle ends with nothing in hand: an ally or an opponent closed the gap first. **Seconds aimed at a level with no room left** is the third, and it is a routing failure rather than a race — the shipped greedy filters a full level out of its own aim list, so it reads zero, and `npm run verify` holds the column with an arm that is ordered to keep aiming at L4. The bottom rows are the ALGAE supply read against its own exhaustion, next to the piece-count check that already closes every one of the 126 CORAL and 18 ALGAE §6.3.4 stages.


## ROBOT work

The same bookkeeping seen from one machine. The second-to-last column adds up the seconds and the feet each action took: a ROBOT at ten seconds of `fetch-coral` over two hundred feet is standing in a long queue, and one at fifteen seconds of `place` over two hundred feet is driving far to put a CORAL away. The feet are measured after the REEF has pushed the machine back out, so a car pinned against the structure books nothing — which is the difference between this column and a speed integral.


## Tab: bench

Mean ± standard error, and the distribution that mean is summarising. The button says which of the two batches you are about to run: **Mirror batch** puts one trio on both ALLIANCES, so its margin is the field-leak test and anything it prints is the model, not the strategy; with the mirror off the two ALLIANCES run their own presets, and the margin is a ROBOT comparison — read the two means. The MATCH count and the seed base sit with the run buttons, and a batch keeps replaying the same seeds until the base changes, which is what turns the line at the bottom of this panel into a paired difference rather than two unrelated runs.


## Fouls across the batch

A distribution is otherwise unreadable: the batch can say that the best MATCH was 341 points and give no way to find out what happened in it. So every batch keeps one row per MATCH — its number, its seed, both scores and the foul points it handed out — and this lists the rows at the ends: each side's best and worst, the widest win for each side, and the highest and lowest combined. Press replay and the engine runs that seed again under the settings the batch used. It is the same MATCH, not a similar one: the engine draws its randomness from the seed, and `npm run verify` holds that equality by re-running every flagged row and requiring the scores it prints. Nothing is stored to make this cheap — 600 frame logs would be most of the page's memory, and a row that points at a seed is the same information in nine numbers.


## Tab: rules

Three entries in that table are worth the reason. G409 and G412 are blocked by construction: `engine.js` refuses a second CORAL or a second ALGAE (`if (rb.coral >= 1) return`) and a placement is only legal with the BUMPERS inside the ALLIANCE'S own REEF ZONE, so the action set cannot produce either foul. G420 is blocked for a different reason — the mesh is display geometry, never a collider, and the ROBOT's own deposit (`score-net`) is made from *beside* it, because §6.5.1 only counts an ALGAE that is not touching a ROBOT of that ALLIANCE; nothing in the action set reaches into the opponent's NET either. G410, G411 and G426 are the honest gaps: no de-score, no ALGAE-on-REEF and no collusion behaviour exists, so a team that won the CORAL RP by de-scoring the opponent's REEF is not something this build can lose to.

G419 changed band on the way past. It used to read **reach** — the ANCHORS hang above 5 ft, and R104 caps a ROBOT at 3 ft 6 in — and both halves of that were wrong: R104 measures the STARTING CONFIGURATION, not an elevator in play, and §5.4.1 lists the ANCHOR as including *the upper end surfaces and the inside surfaces of the CAGE pipes*, which in a DEEP CAGE sit 3 to 27 in off the carpet. A ROBOT is beside them every time it climbs. It is now **none**, which is the honest label: the engine's CLIMB is one atomic action, so there is no contact to adjudicate and no CAGE point to lose.

G421 (1 defender at a time) is charged, and it is why the press rule only ever lets one machine of a fleet go across: over `npm run verify`'s 18000 replay frames of 24 MATCHes, **144** held a single ROBOT across the centreline with its bumpers clear of both zones and none held a second, so two greedy fleets give the clause nothing to do. Drive two machines across on purpose and it reads 52 fouls over 2 ROBOTS, 2 of them escalating MINOR then MAJOR every 3 s as §7.4.3 says — that is guard 23b.

§7.4.4's three clauses are mutually exclusive by the manual's own note, so one collision is one call and only the most punitive one: G422 needs a COMPONENT outside the ROBOT PERIMETER, which no machine in this build has (the box R104 allows and nothing on it), so it is blocked by construction; G423 is damage or functional impairment, whether deliberate (item A) or simply by initiating contact (item B); and G424 needs the tip to be *deliberate*, because "tipping as an unintended consequence of normal ROBOT to ROBOT interaction" is explicitly not a violation. Both G423 and G424 are MAJOR FOUL + YELLOW CARD, or + RED CARD if the opponent cannot drive — and §6.6.2 makes a second YELLOW in the same MATCH a RED, which DISABLES that ROBOT for the remainder. Guard 23 drives a pressing fleet with the damage floor dropped to 20 in/s and the roll forced, and requires all of it: one call per collision, a card on every one, an impairment on the victim, a DISQUALIFICATION, and none of it with `contactFouls` off.

Neither baselines nor the shipped mirror ever commit a foul at all — greedy does not drive into an opponent, so across the guard batch the referee table is empty by strategy rather than by construction. That made `foulsOff` an unfalsifiable claim until `npm run verify` grew a guard that runs a fleet which does foul: one ALLIANCE parked, the other pressing the nearest car, over eight presses, and the build fails unless the ON direction charges (it reads 1035 fouls, `G405×15 · G403×110 · G421×760 · G427×7 · G425×141 · G423×2`, worth 6106 points). The same guard is what proves G425 escalates MINOR to MAJOR on the second 3 s of an uncorrected PIN, which is the one severity in Table 6-3 that depends on how long the violation has already run.


## Tab: data

Field dimensions, element sizes and heights, point values, ranking-point definitions, foul severities, the 15 s AUTO / 135 s TELEOP clock, the 126 CORAL and 18 ALGAE staging, G409's one-CORAL-one-ALGAE carry limit, R103's 115 lb and R104's 120 in perimeter / 42 in height. Each is quoted with its clause in the tables above. The HUMAN PLAYER's part is rules too: 1 NET and 1 PROCESSOR per ALLIANCE (§5.4.2, §5.5), an ALGAE through a PROCESSOR rolls into the opponent's PROCESSOR AREA (§5.5), a PROCESSOR AREA holds at most 4 ALGAE (G435), no ALGAE may be entered during AUTO (G404), and only a HUMAN PLAYER may put them in (G433B). Scoring them is a different list: §6.1 gives both the PROCESSOR and the NET to the ROBOT, and gives the NET to the HUMAN PLAYER as well once the opponent has passed a ring through their PROCESSOR. The HUMAN PLAYER's throws are the biggest single mechanism in this column: turning them off costs **19.7 ± 2.3** a MATCH over 24 paired seeds, 22/24 scores different. The drivetrain does buy throughput too, and `npm run chassis` prices it as a paired mirror on two seed bases: every ROBOT on tank instead of swerve costs **25.7 ± 2.0** and **23.2 ± 2.1** points an ALLIANCE a MATCH (all-swerve **273.6 ± 2.7** against all-tank **248.2 ± 2.9** on the 9000 base), because a machine that cannot strafe spends half a trip backing up and turning. Mecanum, which strafes at 0.62 of its forward rate, is level with swerve (**+1.3 ± 2.1** and **+2.9 ± 3.0**): at these tempos partial strafing is already enough, so the cliff the model finds is at "no sideways at all", not at "some" — and none of the three has a seat advantage to report, because each one's own mirror margin (swerve **1.5 ± 3.0**, tank **2.0 ± 4.7**, mecanum **0.6 ± 3.7**) is inside its own error bar. All of these fractions are estimates — R103 and R104 say nothing about drivetrains.

A mirror MATCH used to be one process played twice: the same trio, a point-symmetric field, a deterministic tick. With **Machine spread** at 0 and a CORAL that cannot miss it still very nearly is, and over the guard's 576 paired seeds that arm ties outright **36** times — not a finding about the game, just the model having no way to tell the two sides apart. Two things take that apart: every ROBOT draws one tempo factor per MATCH (±20% by default, the **Machine spread** field), and each HUMAN PLAYER draws its own form. On the shipped arm, where a miss is its own draw per alliance, the spread takes the mean |margin| from **9.1 to 11.3** (**2.23 ± 0.50**, ties **37 → 30**); on that risk-off arm it widens it too (**2.98 ± 0.44**) while the ties go **36 → 35**, which is the count being noise rather than a finding — so the guard asserts the |margin| and not the ties. Over the 96 seeds `npm run measure` sweeps, ±0% reads **9.1 ± 0.9**, ±10% **10.0 ± 1.1**, ±20% **11.0 ± 0.9** and ±40% **16.3 ± 1.1**, with exact ties 10 → 9 → 3 → 2 across those arms. The three real 2025 Einstein Field finals margins were 11, 14 and 17. The results table prints the factor next to each ROBOT, so you can see which alliance got the slower machines.

The three real 2025 Einstein Field finals were 295-281, 274-285 and 276-293 (margins 14, 11, 17), so the shipped ±20% now sits inside that band and ±40% above its top — a bigger claim about two copies of one design than is worth making. What keeps the two sides from separating further is that both still drain the REEF's *branches* — the generic trio fills 11.9/12.0/11.9 of the three L2-L4 rows over the 24-MATCH mirror's 48 alliance-sides — and the difference between them is mostly the TROUGH and the ALGAE columns, which is what the placement model and the NET reach leave short (6.1 of its 24 against 9.7 when nothing can fall off).

The other half of the symptom was the page itself: **Play one match** replays whatever seed is in the field, so clicking it twice is the same MATCH down to the last tick. **New match** rolls a fresh seed and plays it, and the status chip names the seed you are watching so a result you want to keep can be replayed or exported.

A CORAL STATION sits in the pocket where the ALLIANCE WALL meets the guardrail, three ROBOTS can all want the same one, and nothing in the engine or the baseline ever said "give up". So a machine would arrive, be pushed back by its own teammates, drive in again, and hold that spot — one of them still carrying a CORAL it had aimed at a branch for eighty seconds. Measured on the build where that was found, the 48-MATCH batch finished its weakest ALLIANCE on **13** of the 60 CORAL the field holds and its widest mirror gap was **132** points, between two identical trios. No 2025 field ever produced that; the Einstein finals were 17 points apart at their widest.

Now a ROBOT that is told to go, is more than 22 in from its waypoint, and closes less than 4 in over three seconds backs out along its own heading for 0.6 s and re-decides — which is what a driver does. Same batch on this engine, as `npm run verify` prints it guard by guard: the manoeuvre is asked for **9.1** times a MATCH where `unstickOff` is asked for **0** times, and the tail it used to lift is level — the weakest ALLIANCE of the 96 ends on **31** of the 60 CORAL the field holds in **both** arms, the widest mirror gap is **48** points with the escape against **38** without it, and the 48-MATCH batch averages **537.0** against **535.3**. #124's berth, #125's loose CORAL and #126's branch claim all moved the machines that used to pile up; nothing here says which of them did it, and the fire count only went 10.2 → 9.1, so the two direction clauses this guard used to carry on the extremes are gone and what is left is an anti-harm pair — the escape must not COST the fleet and must not LOWER its worst case — with the tail printed rather than claimed. More than **40** backs-outs a MATCH would mean the engine had started driving through the manoeuvre instead of repairing with it.

The same reasoning is in the baseline for the REEF: a ROBOT that has held a CORAL for four seconds against a branch pushes on to the next level down rather than parking in front of it, because the highest free branch is not always the reachable one.

§6.5.1 pays an L2-L4 CORAL only "if the BRANCH is inside the volume of the CORAL", and a 4 in BRANCH inside §5.7.1's 11 in piece leaves about 3.5 in of slop. So a placement is now two things: a per-level **action time** (`placeSec` — 0.4 s on the TROUGH, 0.65 at L2, 1.0 at L3, 1.4 at L4 on the generic trio, and editable on each ROBOT card) and a per-level **chance of not staying on** (`placeDrop`, `L1 0.02 / L2 0.06 / L3 0.13 / L4 0.22`, rising with how far off the branch the machine was facing when it started pushing). The push does not begin until the ROBOT is actually lined up — 26° of aim on the swerve trio, 20° on the tank machine, because at 45° off a car a foot out points 11 in wide of the pipe — and the unaligned seconds are spent turning, which is where half of the cost is. **CORAL can miss** is the switch; with it off nothing falls and no time is paid for a swing.

What that is worth (n=24 mirror MATCHes, seedBase 9000): the TROUGH goes from **9.7 of its 24 filled to 6.1** while L2/L3/L4 come down from **12.0/12.0/12.0 to 11.9/12.0/11.9**, and **9.9 CORAL an ALLIANCE-MATCH** ends up back on the carpet against 0.0. Paired on identical seeds the model costs an alliance **−9.0 ± 3.3** points a MATCH, 24/24 scores different, and **−16.8 ± 2.2** on a second seed base, 24/24. The column that goes short is the one the manual scores by contact rather than by slot, which is the reading TBA's playoff ledger gives too: its 2025cmptx alliance-sides averaged 15.1 trough CORAL, this fleet 6.1. One knob inside the model is worth naming: a ROBOT that has been held off a branch for 4 s aims one level lower, and turning that retry-giving-up off moves the same arms to 5.8 trough and 11.9 L2, with 9.4 CORAL fallen against the shipped 9.9 — a wash, roughly, and it stays because a machine parked in front of a blocked branch with a full gripper is not scoring either way. `npm run verify` holds the geometry: on the golden seed 82 placements were booked, every one begun within 26° of its branch, and 16 of them did not stay on; `placementRisk: false` hands back the replay this round's ablation was re-based to (`743f27e7`, 265-283, and the arm under it is the one `npm run measure` prints with **0** fallen CORAL).

Two mechanisms the last round of bugs asked for, both switches on the advanced column. **Steer around the REEF**: the hexagon is the only solid thing on the carpet, and when the straight line from a machine to its waypoint crossed a face, the build drove into it, got pushed out, and re-bid the same piece 0.1 s later. `npm run measure` counts that as time spent *pinned and blocked* — three seconds or more against a face with the waypoint more than 22 in away on the far side — and the engine before the fix spent **3.67%** of its ROBOT-seconds that way, in **9.0** episodes a MATCH, the longest of them **39.6 s**, with the pile-up escape firing **14.9** times a MATCH to clean up after it. Diverting *every* blocked trip cures the symptom (**0.33%**, longest **6.0 s**) and costs the named preset nearly three CORAL an ALLIANCE — Johnson falls from **57.5** to **55.0** against the recorded 59 — because a fleet working its own REEF has a hexagon on the line almost always. Waiting **0.6 s** for the machine that presses and *stays* pressed is what ships: **0.49%** of ROBOT-seconds, **1.9** episodes a MATCH, longest **9.2 s**, unstick **10.8** a MATCH, and Johnson at **57.9**. Waiting longer is no better (1.2 s reads **0.78%** and a 9.2 s longest stretch, and Johnson 57.7), and the berth width has a floor and then stops mattering — 4, 8 and 14 in read **0.54%**, **0.49%** and **0.54%** with longest stretches of **8.4**, **9.2** and **9.2** s, while a 2 in berth leaves a **36.0 s** one behind it at **0.88%**. `npm run verify` holds the geometry itself: all 24 through-the-REEF trips (both REEFs, six faces, on and 20 in off the line) are handed a point standing **60.5 in** out from the structure on its far side of the travel line, and none of the six tangential trips is diverted; the swing engages on **4.36%** of robot-seconds as shipped against **27.69%** when every blocked trip goes around. The fix is worth **+4.67 ± 2.51** own-side points over the engine that drove straight into it (22/24 of 24 paired MATCHes), and read from the four seat and seed-base combinations it is **+4.67 ± 2.51**, **+3.29 ± 2.21**, **+1.96 ± 2.06** and **−0.25 ± 3.47** — three of the four positive, the fourth a wash, and none clearing a bar of its own.

**CORAL falls out on the drive**: a piece held against a branch, or carried by a machine that launches and stops at full throttle, comes loose without a placement ever being attempted. The manual bills a piece that is not held and never says how it fell, so the rate is an *estimate* and a sweep that `npm run measure` prints (n=24 an arm): at **0.005** a second the engine shakes **0.83** CORAL a MATCH loose and the arm prices it at **+1.25 ± 2.39** own-side points (21/24 scores differ), at the shipped **0.01** it is **2.04** and **+1.50 ± 2.48** (24/24), at **0.02** it is **3.46** and **+0.58 ± 2.43** (23/24). So the pieces come off the hopper and the batch's own noise swallows what they cost — it is a tax on the MATCH rather than a re-shape of it — and switching it off spends no random number at all, so the replay under it is the engine this was added to.

**Two allies, one branch** (#126): the engine books a BRANCH when the placement *finishes*, so until that tick nobody owns it, and all three machines of an ALLIANCE aimed at the nearest free pipe on the level they had been given. The ledger cannot see it — 36 branch slots a side get filled anyway — so the whole cost is the drive the last machine wasted. The engine now counts the overlap directly: **27.3** ROBOT-seconds an ALLIANCE-MATCH spent aimed at a pipe another ally already had, over **33.1** separate stretches, down to **9.3** s and **9.2** stretches once a branch belongs to whoever started driving to it first (the count is one increment per 0.1 s decision tick over allies beyond the first, so it is not wall-clock seconds of the MATCH; the planner reads 23.7 → 9.7, and `npm run verify` repeats it on its own batch at 26.9 → 8.8 s).
